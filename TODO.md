## Soon: robustness and correctness

- [ ] <a id="v3proto"></a>**Build and test a P1-BASE v3.0 prototype.** Assembled boards and printed enclosures are prepared in the JLCPCB cart; the order waits for the stacking header J1 (Samtec ESQ-108-24-G-D, C21241050, pre-ordered), which then has to be added to the PCBA. Check the points listed in [docs/design-v3.md](docs/design-v3.md#10-open-points-to-verify): stacking-header fit, RJ12 jack height and pinout, TP4056 in parallel with the CoreInk charger, CoreInk `5V_OUT` behaviour, and reading DSMR 2–5 meters (including Landis+Gyr E350/E360) with common P1 readers on the second socket. On the printed enclosure: the snap fit of the lid, the lid key (it must stop a turned lid at first touch), and the pull-off force of the module without a skirt (16 bus contacts plus the crush ribs of the LEGO posts), including a pull on the RJ12 cables. Also affects: needforheat-p1-reader-firmware. Cost at 5/10/30/100 boards: [README](README.md#cost) (JLCPCB quotes of 16 November 2026; at 100 boards JLCPCB had only 62 PCA9633 (U5) in stock). The BatteryBASE prototype (PCBA and prints) can go in the same order.
- [x] <a id="batteryflush"></a>**BatteryBASE enclosure flush with the CoreInk outline.** Done on 16 November 2026 in needforheat-battery-base-hardware (PR #3): CoreInk outline (40 × 56 mm, R3), sliding lid, cradle; fit check clean on a CoreInk and on a P1-BASE v3. The BatteryBASE PCBA (5 pieces) is in the JLCPCB cart. Any BatteryBASE prints in the cart from before 16 November are the old design: replace them with the current `enclosure/fabrication/*.stl` (tray, lid and cradle, $ 4.52 per set) before ordering ([v3proto](#v3proto)).
- [ ] <a id="forces"></a>**Force path when plugging in and pulling off.** Nothing in the enclosure holds the PCB down: it rests on the tray's corner blocks, the lid's corner pillars end 5.3 mm above it and the J1 cut-out leaves 0.25 mm around the socket (values from `enclosure/kuip.py`). (1) *Pushing the P1-BASE onto the CoreInk* by its lid: the force reaches the PCB only through the lid's tongues, which rest 0.2 mm above the RJ12 jacks, so the jacks carry the 16 bus contacts plus the crush ribs. (2) *Pulling a module (e.g. a BatteryBASE) off the P1-BASE* pulls J1 and the PCB up until the jacks hit the tongues; the lid's snap fit then takes the whole pull-off force and may let go, before the module comes off. (3) *Pulling the P1-BASE off the CoreInk* by its tray is fine: the corner blocks lift the PCB. Fix: something rigid between the lid and the PCB (e.g. the corner pillars down to 0.1 mm above the PCB corners, or pins of the lid on the PCB beside J1), so that tray, lid and PCB form one block in both directions; check with the printed prototype ([v3proto](#v3proto)). The BatteryBASE solves the same problem with an insert that clamps its PCB between the lid and the corner blocks (needforheat-battery-base-hardware, `enclosure/README.md`).
- [ ] <a id="hat"></a>**HAT units on the CoreInk's top edge.** M5Stack sells dozens of HATs for the StickC series (sensors, proto board, servo, speaker, battery); several are listed for the CoreInk too, e.g. the 18650C battery HAT. A HAT is about 24 mm wide and 8–24 mm deep and plugs into the 2.54 mm 8-pin header on the CoreInk's top edge; the P1-BASE puts its J2 socket, the meter cable and LED1's window on the same edge, and the tray's top wall is open there for the J2 plug. Check with one or two real HATs (e.g. ENV III, Proto) whether the HAT's body reaches behind the CoreInk's back plane and collides with the tray, the J2 plug or the LED window; if so, move the cut-out or LED1.
