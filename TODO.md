- [ ] <a id="forces"></a>**Force path when plugging in and pulling off.** *Partly done (17 November 2026, design review):* the lid now has feet on three PCB corners (0.1 mm above the PCB), so (1) and the PCB part of (2) are solved; the snap fit still carries the pull-off force in (2), so the prototype order has two lids (bump 0.35 and 0.5 mm) to compare. Original analysis: nothing in the enclosure held the PCB down: it rests on the tray's corner blocks, the lid's corner pillars end 5.3 mm above it and the J1 cut-out leaves 0.25 mm around the socket (values from `enclosure/kuip.py`). (1) *Pushing the P1-BASE onto the CoreInk* by its lid: the force reaches the PCB only through the lid's tongues, which rest 0.2 mm above the RJ12 jacks, so the jacks carry the 16 bus contacts plus the crush ribs. (2) *Pulling a module (e.g. a BatteryBASE) off the P1-BASE* pulls J1 and the PCB up until the jacks hit the tongues; the lid's snap fit then takes the whole pull-off force and may let go, before the module comes off. (3) *Pulling the P1-BASE off the CoreInk* by its tray is fine: the corner blocks lift the PCB. Fix: something rigid between the lid and the PCB (e.g. the corner pillars down to 0.1 mm above the PCB corners, or pins of the lid on the PCB beside J1), so that tray, lid and PCB form one block in both directions; check with the printed prototype ([v3proto](#v3proto)). The BatteryBASE solves the same problem with an insert that clamps its PCB between the lid and the corner blocks (needforheat-battery-base-hardware, `enclosure/README.md`).
- [ ] <a id="hat"></a>**HAT units on the CoreInk's top edge.** M5Stack sells dozens of HATs for the StickC series (sensors, proto board, servo, speaker, battery); several are listed for the CoreInk too, e.g. the 18650C battery HAT. A HAT is about 24 mm wide and 8–24 mm deep and plugs into the 2.54 mm 8-pin header on the CoreInk's top edge; the P1-BASE puts its J2 socket, the meter cable and LED1's window on the same edge, and the tray's top wall is open there for the J2 plug. Check with one or two real HATs (e.g. ENV III, Proto) whether the HAT's body reaches behind the CoreInk's back plane and collides with the tray, the J2 plug or the LED window; if so, move the cut-out or LED1.
